Cabinet math

A run of cabinets ordered from a sketch ends up a box short, a shelf pin line that drifts, or a sheet cut into expensive firewood. Cabinet math turns the wall and a labeled box width into the cabinet count and the filler, turns the box height and a labeled spacing into the shelves, and turns the sheet, a labeled panel and the saw kerf into the cut plan with an honest offcut. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Box plan - the wall at a labeled box width becomes the cabinet boxes and the filler to split between the ends.
  • Shelf plan - the box height at a labeled spacing becomes the shelf count and the gap left at the top.
  • Sheet plan - the sheet at a labeled panel with a saw kerf becomes the panel count and the honest offcut.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: a 60 cm cabinet box, a 30 cm shelf spacing, a 3 mm saw kerf - all illustrative defaults you can change. Real cabinets carry dishes, kids and local rules about anchoring. This is planning arithmetic, not a structural design: measure the real wall and let the manufacturer specs and a qualified installer govern.
